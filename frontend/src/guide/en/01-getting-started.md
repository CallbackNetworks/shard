# Getting started

Shard keeps track of work across the different parts of your life — a job, a
freelance client, a side project — without mixing them together.

If you have used a to-do app, most of this will be familiar. Three ideas are worth
five minutes up front, because everything else in the app is built from them.

## 1. A project is a box that holds work

Nothing exists on its own here. Tasks, labels, sprints, decisions and share links
all live inside a project. So the first thing to do is make one.

## 2. Boxes go inside boxes

A project can sit inside an *identity* ("Work", "Freelance"), and an identity can
sit inside something bigger. Progress adds up through that nesting: if a project
holds three sub-projects, its progress bar counts all of their tasks too. You never
have to build that nesting — one project with tasks in it is a complete, working
setup — but it is there when you want it.

## 3. Everything is the same kind of thing underneath

A project, a task, a decision, a person — under the hood they are all *nodes*, and
the lines between them are *relations*. You can ignore this entirely and the app
works fine. It matters when you want something the app did not come with: you can
add your own kind of item ("client", "invoice", "repository") and it behaves like a
built-in one everywhere. See the chapter on data and item types.

## Your first five minutes

![The Overview](/guide/01-overview.png)

1. **Make a project.** On the Overview, click *New project*. Give it a name.
   That is the only required field.
2. **Add a few tasks.** Open the project and click *New issue*. A title is enough —
   you can add a due date and a priority later, or never.
3. **Move one along.** Click a task's status to change it, or switch to the *Board*
   tab and drag the card into another column.
4. **Come back to the Overview.** The task you started now appears under *In
   Motion*, and the counts at the top have moved.

That is the whole loop. Everything else in this guide is an addition to it.

## Getting around quickly

![The command palette](/guide/26-command-palette.png)

- `Ctrl-K` (or `Cmd-K`) opens search — it finds tasks, projects, decisions, people.
- `G` then `P` opens the same box as a **project switcher**, most recently visited
  first. This is the fastest way to move between projects.
- `C` creates a task from anywhere. `/` jumps to the search box.
- `?` shows every shortcut, plus a button to walk through whatever page you are on.

## When you are lost

Two things will always help, and they are both permanent fixtures:

- **The compass button at the bottom-left** walks you through the page you are
  currently looking at, pointing at each control in turn. A dot on it means you have
  not taken that page's tour yet.
- **This guide**, always in the left menu, near the bottom. It cannot be hidden.
